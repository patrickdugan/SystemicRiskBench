# Traditional-finance replay catalog

This catalog supplies 18 two-stage mechanism replays spanning the 1980s through 2023. It is a starting historical corpus, not a licensed price database, a calibrated reconstruction of order books, or an exhaustive list of crises. Japan's bubble and banking crisis, the dot-com unwind, the 2015 equity/ETF dislocation, the 2014 Treasury flash rally and the 2019 repo episode are useful next additions.

Every record separates agent-visible stage observations from evaluator-only outcomes, source attribution, reasoning anchors and counterfactual interventions. Later stages add new information. Observations that depend on a fictional participant's internal state are explicitly labeled synthetic. There are no numerical proxy balance sheets or invented historical price series in this catalog. Numeric simulations should supply separately labeled parameters with units, horizons and provenance.

All cases are marked `retrospective_reconstruction` and `point_in_time_archive_verified=false`. Their cutoffs are benchmark decision markers. They do not certify the publication time, availability or correctness of every detail in a contemporaneous information set. `event_date_precision` identifies month/year markers that must not be read as exact event dates. Day-level packets assume the listed announcement has already occurred; no intraday timestamp is implied unless one is explicitly given.

The sources are official institutional histories, original academic research, regulator investigations or participant accounts. Federal Reserve History is an official institutional retrospective, rather than a contemporaneous primary document; that distinction remains in each source's `kind`. The January 15, 2015 SNB press release is contemporaneous. Other records must not expose hindsight causal explanations as facts known to the historical agent. The source registry was checked with browsing on November 16, 2026.

For the 2010 flash crash, a synthetic execution-role variant may know its own algorithm, but a public-observer variant must not know the initiating trader's private rule. For Archegos, synthetic broker limit breaches and collateral conditions are role premises: FINMA's 2023 internal findings were not public in March 2021. For SVB, later aggregate withdrawal totals, supervisory conclusions and March 12 protection of uninsured deposits must not leak into March 8 decisions. For LTCM, the September 23 recapitalisation was privately funded; the Fed facilitated it rather than lending the recapitalisation money.

Use at least two tracks. The named history track evaluates analysis of a known episode. The identity-masked track removes dates, names, titles, citations and distinctive magnitudes and tests mechanism-preserving synthetic variants. Masking cannot erase a model's training knowledge. Named-history scores should never be presented as evidence of ex ante prediction. Split by episode and mechanism family before generating variants so sibling variants cannot cross the train/test boundary.

Score causal risk decomposition, honest information requests, strategy and architecture self-critique, executable decisions, uncertainty and justified revision. Do not reward merely predicting the realised outcome or always refusing risk. A defensible initial decision can lose money. Require an explicit horizon, loss sign, probability measure and conditioning assumptions before CVA/CVaR calculations. Settlement principal exposure, replacement costs and collateral liquidity calls need separate accounting; realised default loss must not be double-counted with an expected CVA provision.

For systemic tests, separately vary: agent count with total capital fixed; agent count with per-agent capital fixed; policy overlap; common signal timing; dealer capacity; leverage and collateral calls; and collateral-transfer latency. Agent count alone is not a systemic exposure measure. Counterfactual interventions in this catalog are benchmark designs, not claims about what historically would have happened. Formal verification can establish accounting or policy invariants under stated assumptions; it cannot certify assumptions about future depth, enforceability or coordinated agent behaviour.

Representative source anchors are the [Federal Reserve's 1987 history](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1987), the [SEC/CFTC flash-crash report](https://www.sec.gov/files/marketevents-report.pdf), [Khandani and Lo's original quant-crowding research](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/august07b_2.pdf), the [FSB March 2020 review](https://www.fsb.org/2020/11/holistic-review-of-the-march-market-turmoil/), and the [Bank of England's LDI case study](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2023/2023/financial-stability-buy-sell-tools-a-gilt-market-case-study). Each JSON case includes the precise sources and their scope of support.
